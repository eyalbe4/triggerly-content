Free stock alert app for Android: 7 things to check first

"Free" means very different things in stock alert apps. Some cap you at a handful of alerts, some show ads in the middle of your watchlist, and some only do one-time price targets. Run through these seven checks before you trust an app with the levels you care about.

1. How many alerts do you get for free?

This is the big one. Several popular charting and alert apps limit free users to a few active alerts and sell the rest as a subscription. That's fine for one or two stocks and frustrating for a real watchlist. Look for the alert limit on the pricing page, not the marketing page.

2. What can trigger an alert?

Price targets are table stakes. Ask whether the app can also alert on:

  • percent change (from the open, the prior close, or a reference price)
  • volume above normal (a volume spike)
  • moving-average crosses (SMA and EMA)
  • RSI, MACD and Bollinger Bands
  • new highs and lows
  • trailing stops
  • several conditions at once

3. Where does the alert arrive?

A push notification is easy to miss when your phone is face down or on Do Not Disturb. Email gives you a record; Telegram reaches every device you're signed into. The best setup is usually two channels for the alerts that matter most.

4. One-time or repeating?

If you trade around the same level, you want repeating alerts. If you set a buy price, you want it to fire once and stop.

5. Are there ads?

Ads in a finance app aren't just annoying. They often push trading products and brokers. Check reviews for complaints about ads or upsell pop-ups.

6. Which market hours does it cover?

Some apps track pre-market and after-hours trading; many track only the regular session. Know which one you have so a quiet overnight move doesn't surprise you. (Triggerly tracks the regular session.)

7. Does it tell you why the stock moved?

An alert tells you something happened. Context tells you whether to care. Some apps now attach news or an AI summary to a triggered alert so you don't have to open five tabs.
