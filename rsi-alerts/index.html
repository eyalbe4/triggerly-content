RSI alerts: overbought and oversold notifications that don't spam you

The Relative Strength Index (RSI) measures how strong recent gains are compared with recent losses, on a scale from 0 to 100. Readings above 70 are often called overbought and below 30 oversold. An RSI alert pings you when a stock enters or leaves those zones, so you can review it instead of scanning indicators by hand.

Why 70/30 isn't always the right level

In a strong uptrend, a stock can stay above 70 for weeks. In a downtrend, it can sit under 30 and keep falling. Many traders adjust the levels to the stock and the market:

  • 80/20 for volatile stocks, so only the extremes trigger.
  • 70/30 as a general default.
  • 60/40 for steady, slow-moving names.

Triggerly lets you set your own RSI levels, and you can combine RSI with price targets or Volume Spike in a Combined Alert.

Entering vs leaving the zone

"RSI crossed above 70" tells you momentum is strong. "RSI dropped back below 70" tells you it may be fading. For oversold setups, many traders care more about RSI climbing back above 30 than about it first dipping below. Set the alert for the moment you'd actually act.

Pair RSI with something else

RSI on its own produces plenty of false starts. A Combined Alert that requires two conditions is usually more useful:

  • RSI below 30 and price at or above a support level you've marked
  • RSI above 70 and a volume spike (a strong move with participation)
  • RSI crossing back above 30 and price above its 20-day moving average

FAQ

What RSI level should I set an alert at?

70 and 30 are common defaults. Volatile stocks often work better with 80 and 20, and slow-moving stocks with 60 and 40.

Is RSI overbought a sell signal?

Not by itself. Stocks can stay overbought for a long time in strong trends. Treat an RSI alert as a reason to review the stock, not as an instruction.
