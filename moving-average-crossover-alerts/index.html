Moving average crossover alerts: SMA, EMA and the golden cross

A moving average smooths out daily noise so you can see the trend. When price crosses a moving average, or a short average crosses a long one, many traders treat it as a sign the trend may be changing. You don't have to check charts every evening to spot it. A moving-average cross alert does the checking for you.

SMA vs EMA

  • SMA (simple moving average) weights every day in the window equally. It's slower and steadier.
  • EMA (exponential moving average) gives more weight to recent days, so it reacts faster and gives earlier, but noisier, signals.

Common periods

  • 20-day: short-term trend, popular with swing traders.
  • 50-day: medium-term trend; widely watched.
  • 200-day: the long-term trend line many investors use to decide whether a stock is in an uptrend at all.

Golden cross and death cross

A golden cross is when the 50-day moving average crosses above the 200-day. A death cross is the opposite. They're slow signals: by the time they happen, a lot of the move has already happened. They're useful as trend context, not as precise entry points.

Price-cross vs average-cross

Price crossing its moving average happens earlier and more often. One average crossing another is rarer and lags more. A practical setup: alert on price crossing the 50-day for an early heads-up, and treat the slower crosses as confirmation.

Things to keep in mind

  • In sideways markets, price can cross a moving average back and forth many times. Longer periods and combined conditions help.
  • A cross is information, not a trade signal by itself. Check the news and the bigger picture first.

FAQ

What is a moving average crossover alert?

A notification that fires when a stock's price, or a shorter moving average, crosses above or below a moving average such as the 50-day or 200-day.

Should I use SMA or EMA?

EMA reacts faster and gives earlier signals with more false alarms; SMA is slower and steadier. Many traders use EMA for short periods and SMA for the 50- and 200-day.
